---
title: "Browser wallet"
description: "How `scriipture deploy --browser` signs transactions with MetaMask."
---

`scriipture deploy --browser` deploys without ever touching a private key on disk. It spawns a short-lived local HTTP server, opens a signing page in your browser, and waits for your wallet to sign the transaction.

## Flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="CLI spawns bridge">
    A local server starts on `http://localhost:7654` and stays up for about 30 seconds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Browser opens">
    Scriipture opens `http://localhost:7654` in your default browser. The page renders the deploy payload (bytecode, chain, args).
  </Step>
  <Step title="You sign">
    Click **Sign and send** in your wallet. MetaMask, Rabby, Frame, Coinbase Wallet, and any EIP-1193 provider work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="CLI receives receipt">
    The browser posts the receipt back to the CLI, the bridge shuts down, and the deployed address prints.
  </Step>
</Steps>

```bash
npx scriipture deploy --browser --chain base-sepolia
```

<Info>
  The bridge runs only during a deploy. It binds to `127.0.0.1` and shuts down after the receipt is received or after ~30 seconds.
</Info>

<Warning>
  If `localhost:7654` is already bound, use `--bridge-port <port>` to override.
</Warning>

## When to use it

- You're on a workstation with MetaMask, and you don't want a hot key on disk.
- You're deploying from a Ledger or Trezor via MetaMask.
- You're pairing with a teammate and want them to approve the transaction.

For CI or scripted deploys, use [`deploy`](/commands/deploy) with a keystore instead.
